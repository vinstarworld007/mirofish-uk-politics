# UK scenario 2: Brexit ten years on, rejoin or reset?

Upload this together with `00-uk-politics-2015-2026.md`.

**Prediction question to paste into MiroFish:**
"Ten years after the 2016 referendum, Prime Minister Andy Burnham proposes a major new UK–EU agreement covering a customs union for goods, a youth mobility scheme and closer alignment with EU rules, but not full rejoining. How do Leave and Remain voters, the parties, the media and business react online over the following months, does support for rejoining the EU grow, and does the plan help or hurt Labour?"

## Hypothetical policy being tested (not real)
- A UK–EU customs union for goods, ending most border checks on trade, including between Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
- A youth mobility scheme letting 18–30 year olds live and work in the EU and UK for up to 3 years.
- Dynamic alignment with EU food and product standards.
- No return to free movement in general, no EU membership and no euro.

## Stakeholders and positions
- **Labour/Burnham:** frames it as "fixing Brexit, not reversing it" to grow the economy.
- **Conservatives/Badenoch:** call it "Brexit betrayal by the back door" while trying to win back Leave voters from Reform.
- **Reform UK/Farage:** call it surrender and use it to recover after the donation scandals.
- **Restore Britain/Lowe:** call for a hard break and mass deportations, and attack Reform as too soft.
- **Lib Dems and Greens:** welcome it but say it doesn't go far enough; the Lib Dems call for a path back to the single market.
- **SNP and Plaid Cymru:** say Scotland and Wales should be able to rejoin as independent nations.
- **Business groups (CBI, British Chambers of Commerce, farmers, exporters):** mostly supportive.
- **Fishing communities and some manufacturing towns:** sceptical.
- **Young voters:** strongly supportive of youth mobility.
- **Media:** the Telegraph, Daily Mail and GB News are hostile; the Guardian, FT and The New European are supportive.

## Variables you can inject
- The EU demands a role for the European Court of Justice in the deal.
- A poll shows 60% would vote to rejoin the EU.
- A major car maker announces new UK investment conditional on the deal.
- Farage calls for a new referendum to block it.
