A UK politics lab where AI voters argue it out

MiroFish turns a briefing document into a crowd of AI agents with their own views. They post, reply and change their minds on simulated Twitter and Reddit, and a report agent writes up what happened. I've set it up for British politics, from the 2015 election and Brexit through to October 2026.

Architecture

From briefing paper to a simulated public

Two programs run on your PC, and they call two cloud services. The red dots follow your document through the pipeline. The green dots are calls out to the cloud.

1

Ontology

The AI reads your briefing and the question you ask. It decides which kinds of actors matter: parties, politicians, media outlets, influencers and voter groups.

2

Knowledge graph

Zep Cloud stores every person and organisation from the document, and how they connect. For example, Farage leads Reform and the Telegraph criticises Labour.

3

Agents

Each entity becomes an agent with a backstory, opinions, a posting style and followers. The AI also sets the clock: how many simulated hours pass, and when people are active.

4

Simulation

OASIS, a social-network engine from CAMEL-AI, runs two worlds side by side, one like Twitter and one like Reddit. Each round, agents read their feed and decide whether to post, reply, like or follow.

5

Report and interviews

A report agent searches the graph and the posts to answer your question. Afterwards you can interview any agent: ask the Red Wall voter why they switched.

+

Inject and rerun

Change one fact, for example "Farage resigns", and run again. Comparing runs shows what moves opinion, which is more useful than any single forecast.

Running it

Five steps once billing is on

  1. Turn on Gemini billing in Google AI Studio. You keep the same key. Alternatively, add credit to OpenAI and ask me to switch back.
  2. Double-click C:\Users\Home\Apps\MiroFish\Start-MiroFish.bat and leave the black window open.
  3. At localhost:3000, upload 00-uk-politics-2015-2026.md and one scenario file.
  4. Paste the scenario's prediction question, and start with 10–20 rounds.
  5. Read the report, interview a few agents, then inject one change and run again to compare.
Before you run

The free Gemini tier allows 20 requests a day, and one simulation needs several hundred. With billing on, a 15-round run on Gemini Flash should cost well under £1. Treat results as a model of how these groups might argue, not as a poll.
